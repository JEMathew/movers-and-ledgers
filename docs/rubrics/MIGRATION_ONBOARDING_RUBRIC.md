# Migration & Onboarding Rubric

## Purpose

The Migration Reviewer uses this canonical rubric to assess whether the migration journey preserves
accounting meaning, exposes blockers and unsupported behavior, governs consequential decisions, and
connects each stage to verified First Productive Use without claiming unimplemented execution.

Every criterion must be reported with: **Criterion**, **What good looks like**, **Why it matters to
MoveBooks AI**, **Why it matters to the end user**, **Failure mode if missing**, **Evidence / metric**,
and a **0–4 scoring scale**. Scores are criterion-level evidence summaries, not an overall score.

## Criteria

| Criterion | What good looks like | Why it matters to MoveBooks AI | Why it matters to the end user | Failure mode if missing | Evidence / metric | 0–4 scoring scale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source, target, and canonical boundaries | Source facts remain immutable, provider adapters stay at edges, and canonical concepts preserve accounting meaning. | Enables provider-neutral expansion without leaking vendor behavior into core logic. | Users can trust that records are interpreted consistently across providers. | Provider assumptions distort or silently discard source accounting semantics. | Adapter contracts; canonical schemas; source immutability; unsupported-item reports. | **0:** boundary absent; **1:** provider-coupled; **2:** partial canonicalization; **3:** explicit and tested for scope; **4:** validated across representative source/target adapters. |
| Readiness, planning, and dependencies | Deterministic findings flow into an ordered plan whose prerequisites, blockers, risks, checkpoints, actions, and approvals cannot be silently cleared. | Turns assessment evidence into an executable, governed migration strategy. | Users know what must happen, why, and what blocks progress. | Work executes out of order or unresolved readiness problems disappear from the plan. | Plan schema; dependency validation; blocker propagation; transition tests. | **0:** no governed plan; **1:** narrative only; **2:** incomplete dependency/control model; **3:** structured and tested; **4:** validated with representative complex migrations. |
| Mapping and accounting compatibility | Every source-to-target proposal is schema-valid, canonically compatible, evidence-backed, versioned, and appropriately sensitive to account, tax, entity, and configuration rules. | Mapping correctness is central to financial migration quality. | Users avoid misclassified accounts, taxes, parties, products, and settings. | Unsupported or semantically wrong targets are accepted. | Compatibility tests; allowed targets; required fields; duplicate detection; mapping override rate. | **0:** uncontrolled mappings; **1:** suggestion-only checks; **2:** incomplete controls; **3:** authoritative tested controls; **4:** validated across broad golden/customer cases. |
| Human approval and migration handoff | All consequential mappings have attributable decisions; blocked/rejected/pending items stop handoff; approved handoff is versioned and performs no implicit write. | Separates recommendation from authorization and later execution. | Users know exactly what they authorized before data moves. | Cosmetic approval or premature handoff causes unreviewed financial changes. | Approval coverage; actor/time/comment; handoff tests; no-write evidence. | **0:** silent progression; **1:** optional approval; **2:** partial gates; **3:** complete tested gate for scope; **4:** delegated/production authorization validated independently. |
| Blockers, unsupported items, and exception ownership | Unsupported or incomplete data is visible, classified, assigned an action, and cannot be transformed away without policy and approval. | Prevents false readiness and silent data loss. | Users can resolve or explicitly disposition every material issue. | Missing fields, unsupported settings, or rejected items vanish from the journey. | Blocked-state tests; exception inventory; owner/action; resolution and disposition evidence. | **0:** silent loss; **1:** detection without stop; **2:** stop without repair ownership; **3:** explicit stop/action for scope; **4:** end-to-end resolution and regression controls. |
| Lineage, evidence, and audit integrity | Plans, mappings, transformations, decisions, and results retain versioned source/evidence links and immutable production audit intent. | Makes financial outcomes reproducible and defensible. | Users can trace what changed, why, by whom, and from which source fact. | Disputes or failures cannot be reconstructed. | Evidence coverage; checksums; policy/model versions; append-only audit; trace completeness. | **0:** untraceable; **1:** logs only; **2:** attributable local records with durability gaps; **3:** complete trace for declared scope; **4:** durable immutable trace verified through recovery. |
| Execution, validation, and recovery boundary | Only an approved manifest can reach idempotent writes; deterministic reconciliation, exception handling, rollback/recovery, and completion controls are explicit before execution is claimed. | Protects financial truth across the highest-risk stages. | Users are not told a migration succeeded until records and balances are verified. | Partial writes, false reconciliation, or unrecoverable failure. | Manifest contract; idempotency; counts/balances/checksums; rollback tests; exception closure. | **0:** unsafe/false completion; **1:** implied controls; **2:** architecture only; **3:** current slice truthfully stops with a valid future contract; **4:** implemented and production-tested end-to-end. |
| Onboarding and verified First Productive Use continuity | Configuration, access, integrations, training, and the agreed productive task are linked to a versioned FPU contract and customer evidence. | Ensures migration completion produces real customer value. | Authorized users can actually perform the intended job in the target system. | Technical data movement is called success while users remain unable to work. | Onboarding completion; role/access validation; productive-task evidence; time-to-value; support burden. | **0:** completion misclaimed; **1:** onboarding absent from journey; **2:** future boundary only; **3:** explicit contract and handoff continuity; **4:** verified representative FPU outcomes. |

## Interpretation

- Grade current claims, while verifying that future stages remain explicit and cannot be inferred as
  implemented.
- A truthful safe stop may score higher than a partially implemented execution path.
- Never average criteria into a vanity score. Report each score with its evidence, gap, severity, and
  remediation.
- Use P0–P3 from [Reviewer Rubrics](../reviewers/REVIEW_RUBRICS.md).
